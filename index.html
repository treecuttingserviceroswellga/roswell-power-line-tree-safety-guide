Trees Near Power Lines in Roswell, GA: What Homeowners Should Do

A branch does not have to bring a line down to create danger. Wet wood, damaged conductors, unstable trunks, and equipment can create an electrical path before anyone realizes the tree is energized.

Stay back and report the hazard. Never touch a tree, fence, vehicle, or puddle near a downed line. Treat every conductor as energized and contact the utility or emergency services.

Why ordinary pruning rules do not apply

Electricity can arc across a gap, especially when tools, ladders, ropes, or wet branches narrow the distance. A cut limb can swing toward a conductor, and a tree under tension can move in an unexpected direction. Line-adjacent work therefore requires specialized training, equipment, and coordination.

What can a homeowner do safely?

  1. Observe from a distance and keep other people out of the area.
  2. Use the utility's outage or hazard-reporting channel when conductors are involved.
  3. Photograph conditions only from a location that does not require approaching the tree or line.
  4. Wait for the utility to state that the electrical hazard has been controlled before arranging cleanup.

Planning tree work near a service line

Explain the line location, tree lean, broken branches, and access conditions before anyone arrives. A qualified provider can determine whether utility coordination or line-clearance capability is needed. When planning non-emergency work, share those details with a local tree service in Roswell so the site can be evaluated before equipment is positioned.

Reduce future conflicts

Choose low-maturing species beneath overhead lines and reserve taller trees for locations where their mature canopy cannot reach conductors. Periodic inspections can also identify weak unions, dead limbs, or increasing lean before severe weather turns them into an urgent problem.
